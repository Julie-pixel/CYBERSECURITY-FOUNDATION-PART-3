## 1) Become a Hacker

This was a short but motivational room that helped shape my mindset as a cybersecurity learner.

### What I Understood
- Being a “hacker” in cybersecurity means being curious, analytical, and persistent.
- It is less about “breaking things” and more about understanding how systems work and where they fail.
- Ethical behavior is a core part of the journey: permission, legality, and responsibility come first.

### Key Takeaway
I should focus on building strong fundamentals and problem-solving habits, not just tools.

---

## 2) Become a Defender

This room introduced me to the defensive side of cybersecurity and the role of SOC analysts.

### What Defenders Do Daily
- Monitor alerts and logs
- Investigate suspicious activity
- Triage incidents by severity
- Contain and respond to threats
- Document findings and improve detection rules

### What I Learned
- Defense is continuous and fast-paced.
- Analysts must make decisions using evidence from multiple sources.
- Communication and reporting are just as important as technical skill.

---

## 3) Defensive Security Intro

This room showed how defenders detect and stop attacks in real time.

### Core Defensive Concepts
- **Detection:** Identify unusual or malicious behavior from logs/events.
- **Investigation:** Understand what happened, when, and how.
- **Response:** Take action to stop or limit the attack.
- **Improvement:** Update controls to prevent similar attacks again.

### Hands-on Understanding
In the practical part, I observed how a basic attack can be identified and handled through defensive actions.  
This made defensive security feel practical, not just theory.

---

## Challenges I Faced
- Switching mindset from attacker-focused learning to defender-focused learning.
- Understanding how monitoring and response connect in real workflows.

## How I Solved Them
- I compared both perspectives side by side (attacker steps vs defender actions).
- I rewrote the defensive workflow in simple steps: detect → investigate → respond → improve.

---

## Final Reflection
These rooms helped me see that cybersecurity is both offense and defense.  
The attacker mindset helps me understand threats, and the defender mindset helps me protect systems.  
I now have a clearer view of SOC work and real-time threat handling.
